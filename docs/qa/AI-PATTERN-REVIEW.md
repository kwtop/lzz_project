## Adjusted in this pass

- Home: changed the three equal product cards to one featured part with two smaller comparison rows on desktop; grounded transparent part cutouts on a subtle neutral surface. Replaced generic selected-part and inquiry headings with geometry- and task-specific wording. Replaced “Bring the part to life” in the factory preview with a concrete machining step.
- About: reduced the visual dominance of two generated application scenes by making them compact image-and-copy references. Their AI illustration labels remain visible. Moved the archive relationship caveat into a small note below the real people photographs.
- Capability detail: when three photographed cases are relevant, show a complete three-column row rather than an orphaned third card.
- Home hero: identified the generated machining scene as an illustrative visual in the visible baseline.

## Remaining priorities

1. **Hero authenticity.** The dark machining hero is cinematic and generated, so it can still read like a stock manufacturing visual. It is labeled and followed by a real LZZ workshop photograph. A new high-resolution LZZ machining photograph, composed with space for the headline, would be a stronger long-term hero than further image generation.
2. **Catalog sameness.** The 35 refined transparent cutouts are clear but their repeated near-white card treatment creates a rendered-catalog feel. Product galleries should remain refined-only as requested. Add varied real process or batch context at the category level, with explicit separation from any individual SKU, when suitable source photos exist. Review shape-specific titles against original product evidence before changing them.
3. **Application scenes.** The laboratory and hydraulic images are visibly polished AI concepts. Their new smaller placement makes them explanatory rather than proof. Replace them only with verified real application photography, if supplied; do not fabricate customer equipment or results.
4. **Case depth.** The case pages correctly use real process photographs but repeat some shots and lean heavily on generic planning language because drawings, inspection reports and verified measurements are unavailable. The best next improvement is real annotated setup/process material and approved anonymized inspection records, not invented tolerances.
5. **Proof-media quality.** Some real machine and event photos have softer light and color than the catalog renders. Keep their documentary identity. Better source masters or a new factory shoot would improve the cross-page visual consistency; generated factory images must not replace real proof.

Avoid sweeping font or animation changes. The current typography, navigation and inquiry interactions are coherent; the strongest gains come from photographic authenticity, less repetitive card composition and more source-specific engineering detail.
