# B2B Website Design Guidelines

Updated: 2026-10-15.

## Current LZZ project requirements — 2026-10-15

These explicit project requirements override the reusable defaults below where they differ.

- Scope: all 84 current business pages and 404. Preserve URLs, factual constraints, useful anchors, SEO/GEO and inquiry-draft behavior.
- Ordinary content pages must contain 4–7 core narrative sections. Count independent buyer tasks, not every heading, card, FAQ item, navigation or footer. Task utilities may have a documented structural exception without filler.
- Every page must contain at least three distinct, relevant content images. Logos, icons, repeated copies, hidden images and responsive variants do not count. Place them beside the information they explain; do not append an unrelated image strip merely to pass the count.
- Most showcase modules should combine meaningful imagery with useful text. Guides use imagery at the explanation or decision it supports; keep readable technical tables. Forms and recovery actions remain early and prominent.
- Keep suitable real imagery first; enhance useful weak images; generate missing non-evidence visual explanations with ImageGen and actually adopt reviewed outputs. Never synthesize business proof.
- Reduce ornamental subheadings, explanatory microcopy, repeated links, labels and arrows. Preserve necessary conditions and disclosures, but group them with the relevant information.
- Verify every URL's module map and unique content-image count, inspect representative full pages at 1440px and 390px before rollout, then inspect variants and narrow screens. Counts and successful builds alone do not establish visual quality.
- This version replaces the prior Website Design Constitution, archived at `docs/archive/DESIGN-before-image-led-20261015.md`. Baseline and implementation evidence are in `D:/lzz/image-led-rebuild-20261015/`.

### LZZ implementation values

- Inter Variable; body 17px/1.65 on desktop, 16px on mobile. Keep source captions at 14px/1.5 and form inputs at 16px or larger. Text remains selectable HTML.
- Main width 1240px; desktop gutter 40px, mobile 20px, narrow screens 16px. Body copy generally stays within 60–72ch; technical tables may span the content width.
- Base headings: H1 44px/1.14, H2 30px/1.28, H3 21px/1.35. Homepage, form and editorial page variants may use their existing task-specific scale; mobile headings wrap naturally without shrinking body text.
- Primary ink #191b1d, secondary #606266, action red #cf141a, soft surface #f6f6f5, separators #dededb. Dark chapters group related manufacturing content, not alternate mechanically.
- Related items use 16–32px separation, main chapters typically 56–80px desktop and 32–48px mobile. Let actual content determine height; no empty minimum-height filler.
- Product shapes remain complete; evidence photographs retain truthful crops and captions. New conceptual editorial images are 3:2 and normally shown complete. Use one primary image with subordinate details when appropriate rather than three equal images everywhere.
- Guide tables remain readable in HTML, with 16px cell padding and stacked mobile labels. Form actions remain before supporting images on mobile. Privacy-control screenshots use separate mobile captures.

This is a reusable design template. Apply it within the user's requested scope and the project's confirmed design direction. It does not replace applicable AGENTS.md, engineering constraints, business facts, or a project-specific implementation record. A rule in this document is a design requirement, not evidence that a page has already been implemented or verified.

## Goal

Build pages that are visually appealing, trustworthy and comfortable to read. Help buyers understand the product, compare relevant differences, find procurement information, assess evidence and prepare an inquiry.

Use purposeful imagery, clear content relationships, comfortable typography and useful whitespace. Avoid generic slogans, decorative labels, repetitive cards and unnecessary buttons. Do not make pages empty merely to make them look minimal.

## Working Order

Refine one coherent direction in this order:

1. Remove or merge low-value content.
2. Establish module order, layout and reading hierarchy.
3. Select, edit or generate images for the intended compositions.
4. Refine typography, line lengths and spacing.
5. Finish controls, visual details and appropriate motion.
6. Inspect actual desktop and mobile pages, fix problems and verify important interactions.

Do not restart the visual design after each stage. When a direction is already confirmed, deepen it without proposing new styles or requesting approval for every page. Follow explicit user instructions about review and implementation.

## Content and Procurement Value

Every module must answer a buyer's question and have one primary focus. Keep related information together and separate different topics with purposeful spacing.

- Remove repeated slogans, redundant introductions, decorative eyebrow labels, empty advantages and explanations that only repeat the image or heading.
- Preserve product identity, specifications, units, configuration differences, compatibility conditions, limitations, procurement terms, dated evidence and useful internal links.
- Keep functional text: breadcrumbs, field labels, validation errors, privacy information, source notes and necessary qualifications.
- Consolidate repeated facts into an authoritative location on the page. A landing page must still make sense to a first-time visitor.
- Record the original location and final destination of substantive content that is merged or moved. Preserve necessary anchors and inquiry context.
- Never invent specifications, availability, lead times, certifications, customer results or company capabilities to fill a layout.
- Do not simulate simplicity by shrinking text, lowering contrast or placing all useful content in accordions.

## Page Structure by User Task

For this LZZ implementation, ordinary content pages use four to seven main sections with distinct responsibilities. Group related information to meet that structure without filler or loss of necessary content. Document genuine task-page exceptions.

| Page type | Recommended reading order | Image role |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Business and product focus → useful category entry → service scope → real trust evidence → inquiry starting point | Establish one main visual focus; use product and evidence imagery to support subsequent decisions |
| Product directory | Scope → search/filter → comparable products → relevant buying guidance → next step | Clear, consistent product images; no large decorative hero ahead of selection |
| Product family or brand | Range and differences → actual models → selection conditions → inquiry | Adapt to the actual range; a one-model page must not repeat the same model in several modules |
| Product detail | Product and configuration → known facts and unknowns → purchase/application conditions → relevant alternatives → inquiry | Real product views, details and useful context; preserve exact structure and original-image access |
| Services or solutions | Buyer task and scope → service stages → responsibilities and records → necessary questions → next step | Explain distinct stages or applications; align images and copy when services are compared |
| Company or case | Identity or project facts → real records/evidence → relevant capability or outcome → next step | Actual photographs and documents; retain dates, context, attribution and evidence boundaries |
| Guides, comparison and FAQ | Direct answer or search → organized explanation/comparison → conditions and sources → related task | At least three relevant images distributed at the decisions they explain; keep full technical tables |
| Inquiry or contact | Task and expected result → fields/contact options → review or confirmation → next action | Three contextual preparation/evidence images, with fields and contact options remaining prominent |
| Policy, error and utility pages | Necessary information or recovery action | Three useful visuals: actual interface screenshots for actions, or relevant illustrated recovery destinations; no decorative filler |

These are organizational principles, not a template to copy into every page. Missing real cases or evidence must remain missing until supplied.

## Image + Text Composition

For image-led showcase, product and service pages, use image + text relationships wherever they improve understanding and visual rhythm. Aim for imagery to carry much of the initial attention while text explains differences, conditions and next steps.

- Consider a dominant image with concise copy, side-by-side image and text, image above text, a scene paired with key facts, or a small image sequence with short explanations.
- Give important images enough space. Do not use tiny thumbnails beside dense paragraphs.
- Vary image scale according to importance; do not make every image equally large or every module identical.
- Avoid consecutive large text-only sections on showcase pages. First shorten or reorganize the content, then add useful imagery if it has a clear task.
- Use tables for comparable specifications, steps for procedures and comfortable reading widths for articles. Do not turn everything into three-column cards.
- Parallel comparison modules should share image ratios and text baselines. Editorial sequences can use different proportions deliberately.
- Determine mobile reading order explicitly. Do not allow decorative images to displace the product identity, essential facts or form.

The current project requires at least three distinct useful content images per page. Reusing one image several times or adding unrelated images does not make a page complete. Keep utility-page visuals compact and explanatory; preserve all policy and form text in accessible HTML. Most showcase sections should have a purposeful image/text relationship, with variation in scale and composition.

## Active Image Improvement and ImageGen

Image work is part of implementation, not optional decoration. Do not keep an unsuitable image simply because a file already exists.

Inspect the actual source image and its rendered placement before deciding. Identify whether the problem is source quality, canvas ratio, internal whitespace, subject size, lighting, background, content mismatch or the page's sizing/cropping rules.

Choose a concrete treatment for each important image slot:

1. Keep an image that already fits the task and composition.
2. Correct the layout or crop when the source itself is suitable.
3. Edit, extend or clean an existing image with ImageGen when it will materially improve composition, background, lighting or consistency.
4. Regenerate non-evidence imagery when the original cannot adequately serve the layout.
5. Request real photography or documentation when accuracy or proof cannot be preserved by editing.

Prefer active improvement for weak hero images, category imagery, application scenes and service illustrations. Do not stop at a container adjustment when the underlying image still harms the page. Do not generate images merely to increase the count.

Before editing or generating, specify:

- Page, module and the image's information task.
- Actual reference file and whether it is an edit target or a style reference.
- Target aspect ratio, subject position, subject size and surrounding space.
- Text-safe area, if text will overlay the image.
- Mobile presentation and the features that must remain visible.
- Shared lighting, color temperature, background complexity and material treatment.
- Product or evidence details that must not change.

### Accuracy and evidence boundaries

- Real product images must retain structure, proportions, component count, interfaces, configuration and identifying marks. If generation cannot reliably preserve these, keep the real subject and limit changes to permissible background/canvas work, or request a new photograph.
- Never generate substitutes for certificates, customer feedback, real company/factory records, inspections or deliveries.
- Generated scenes may illustrate an application or service, but must not be described as actual projects, facilities or transactions. Preserve any required visible disclosure and accurate alt text.
- Keep names, specifications, units and procurement conditions in readable HTML, not embedded in generated images.
- Preserve source files. Save edited/generated variants separately and document reference, prompt, treatment, adoption or rejection and final usage.
- Inspect the adopted image in the actual page at desktop and mobile sizes. Generation success alone is not acceptance.

## Typography, Layout and Whitespace

Use the project's existing suitable font and confirmed brand palette. Do not change fonts or add accent colors merely to make a refinement look different.

Project-specific values take precedence. Where no system exists, these are starting points to test in actual rendering:

| Element | Desktop starting point | Mobile starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Body | 18px, line-height 1.6–1.7 | 16–18px, line-height 1.6–1.7 |
| General page heading | 36–48px, line-height 1.1–1.2 | 30–36px |
| Section heading | 28–40px | 24–30px |
| Controls and essential parameters | 16–18px | At least 16px for inputs |
| Dates, sources and captions | Around 14px with readable contrast | Do not make smaller merely to fit |
| Main container | Approximately 1200–1280px | Approximately 20px side gutters |
| Long-form reading width | Approximately 60–70ch or 720px | Available width with comfortable gutters |
| Major section spacing | Approximately 56–80px | Approximately 32–48px |
| Related content spacing | Approximately 16–32px | Approximately 16–24px |

Use a small, consistent set of type sizes and weights. Keep headings close to their content. Avoid isolated words, awkward title breaks and long paragraphs spanning the full display width.

Whitespace must show grouping and priority. Avoid oversized empty areas, mechanically tall heroes and spacing added merely to lengthen a page. When content does not fit, revise expression, width and layout before reducing text size.

## Buttons, Forms and Interaction

- Give each main decision area one clear primary action. Secondary navigation usually needs a link, not another filled button.
- Name the result of an action. Avoid vague Learn More labels when the destination can be stated clearly.
- Use one consistent control system. Aim for 48px control height and at least 44px touch targets; document justified narrow-screen exceptions.
- Keep select controls visibly distinguishable from text inputs. Leave room for the selected value and the dropdown indicator.
- Keep field labels near their inputs and separate adjacent fields consistently. Group help text with the fields it describes.
- Put submission consequences next to the action: preparing a brief, opening an email draft and actually sending a message are different outcomes.
- Retain errors, entered values and recovery paths after failure. Verify draft restoration, storage failure and relevant limits when the page supports them.
- Keep keyboard focus visible, dialogs operable and mobile controls reachable. Do not depend on hover alone.
- Remove decorative arrows or icons; retain those needed to communicate navigation, expansion, gallery movement or state.

Use restrained motion for feedback or reading continuity. Preserve user scrolling and provide reduced-motion behavior. Do not add a repeated reveal effect to every module or replace useful content with animation.

## Review and Implementation Workflow

1. Read applicable AGENTS.md, the confirmed plan, current project design rules and relevant components/data. Inspect actual rendered pages.
2. Preserve a recoverable baseline without overwriting existing user changes. Record screenshots, facts, image references, important links, SEO and inquiry behavior.
3. For an existing confirmed direction, identify concrete problems rather than reopening style selection. When independent review is requested and available, use a fresh reviewer with current screenshots and constraints, without previous praise or implementation justifications.
4. Implement one representative page per relevant type with real content and final intended imagery. Check necessary variants before extending shared rules.
5. Fix content relationships, then layout, imagery, typography and controls within the same direction. Make changes in the responsible components and styles rather than accumulating global overrides.
6. Recheck original findings and regressions after changes. Do not turn each review into a new redesign.
7. Update the project's single active DESIGN.md with verified rules and explicit exceptions. Archive the previous version so conflicting active standards do not coexist.

Independent review is distinct from implementation assistance. Do not claim independent review when it was unavailable or the reviewer inherited the implementation's conclusions.

## Acceptance and Delivery

A page is ready when its content is clear, imagery serves its task, layout is comfortable, controls work and the actual rendered result has been inspected. Neither section/image counts nor a successful build are sufficient acceptance criteria.

Verify, in proportion to the changes:

- Matching before/after desktop and mobile viewports, plus a narrow-screen check. A practical baseline is 1440×1000, 390×844 and 320×844, unless the project specifies otherwise.
- Full-page rhythm and readable local details, including important images, headings and form groups.
- No unwanted horizontal overflow, overlap, clipping, broken images or missing primary actions.
- Preserved product facts, units, unknowns, evidence, effective links, SEO and inquiry context.
- Relevant default, selected, empty, invalid, failure and restored states; important keyboard behavior and reduced motion.
- Actual image adoption, consistent treatment within comparable groups and appropriate mobile crops.

Record which URLs were visually inspected, checked for content/function only, inferred from a shared template or not verified. Do not use a few representative screenshots to claim full-site visual coverage.

Deliver a concise change summary, page completion list, same-viewport comparisons, content migration notes, image treatment/adoption list, updated design rules, relevant test results and outstanding real-material needs. Clearly distinguish local preview, staging and production. Do not deploy production without authorization, and do not claim unperformed checks.
